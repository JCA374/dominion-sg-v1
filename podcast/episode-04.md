# Episode 04: Teaching without breaking

We had three hundred and eighty nine verified lessons. In each one, the teacher found a buy that beats our network's own choice, and confirmed it on fresh deals. This episode is about how to put those lessons into the network, the mathematics of the two forces involved, and what happened when I tried.

The student is our best network itself. That was a deliberate choice. An earlier attempt trained a separate small network from scratch, and it never got past fifty percent. Starting from the network that already plays well, we only need to nudge a few decisions.

The first force is the lesson. For each verified position, call the teacher's buy b and our network's buy c. The network gives each a logit, a score. I want the score of b to rise above the score of c. Write d for the difference, the logit of b minus the logit of c. The loss for that position is the verified gain times the softplus of minus d. Softplus is the logarithm of one plus e to the x. When d is very negative, the loss grows like minus d, a steady push. When d is large and positive, the loss fades to zero. Its slope is the logistic function of minus d. So the push is strongest while c still wins, and it relaxes once b is clearly ahead. This is the same form as the Bradley Terry model for comparing two players. Weighting by the verified gain means a lesson worth twenty points pushes ten times harder than one worth two.

The second force is the anchor. The network makes about two hundred decisions per game: playing actions, buying, trashing, discarding, choosing gains. Only buys were labelled. If I only push on buys, the shared layers move, and every other decision moves with them. So I collected eighty four thousand five hundred positions from our network's own games, of every kind. On each, I compare the new network's probabilities with the frozen network's. The measure is the Kullback Leibler divergence. In words: for each option, take the frozen probability, multiply by the log of the frozen probability over the new probability, and add them up. It is zero when nothing changed, and it grows as the new network disagrees. Its gradient on the logits is simply the new probabilities minus the frozen ones. A second anchor term holds the win estimate in place.

The total loss is the lesson plus lambda times the anchor. Lambda decides who wins the tug of war.

With lambda equal to one, the lessons won. On the training lessons, the network chose the teacher's buy ninety eight percent of the time. But on held out lessons, from kingdoms it never saw, only about a quarter moved. And the anchors broke. On its own positions, the new network agreed with the old one only eighty six percent of the time, and only seventy two percent on buys. With a looser label threshold it was worse. That version scored twenty one point seven percent against the original network. It had memorised three hundred buys and forgotten how to play.

With lambda equal to one hundred, the anchors held. Agreement stayed at ninety seven percent overall, ninety four and a half on buys. The divergence fell to about one hundredth. But now the held out lessons barely moved: about one in twenty. Against the original network, on three hundred fresh development kingdoms, it scored fifty two and a half percent. The interval runs from forty nine point three to fifty five point eight. That was the best of five settings I tried on those kingdoms, so it is flattered a little by selection. Lambda three hundred scored fifty one point six.

What does that mean? Three hundred and five training lessons are too few to teach a rule that transfers. Each one is a rare exception: a particular deck, supply and score where a different buy is better. The network can memorise them, but memorising them costs more elsewhere than it gains. The honest summary is that the supervised step made a small change of uncertain value. On the bank, it changed about seven percent of buys, and their average effect was slightly positive, but tiny.

That is why the next step matters. Both branches now get exactly the same reinforcement training, on the same games. If the small nudge helps, branch B should stay ahead of branch A. If it does not, we will know that too.
